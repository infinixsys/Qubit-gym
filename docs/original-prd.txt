Product Blueprint: AI-First Fitness Operating System
Product Requirements Document · PRD v1.0 · October 2026

The product should be designed as a unified fitness operating system for gym businesses and consumers, not merely a gym management app with an AI chatbot added to it.

The platform will combine multi-branch gym management, consumer fitness experiences, adaptive AI coaching, business automation, connected equipment, and a fitness marketplace. It should be commercially viable as a subscription-based SaaS product while allowing the consumer experience to grow into a larger fitness network.

The objective is to create a differentiated product that competes on measurable outcomes, operational intelligence, interoperability, and personalization—not on unsupported claims that existing competitors lack particular features.

1. Executive product definition
Working product name: QubitFit AI

Provisional name only; trademark, domain and brand availability have not been verified.

Product category: AI-powered fitness SaaS, gym ERP, personal fitness platform and connected-fitness ecosystem.

Product vision: Every member gets a personalized fitness journey, every trainer gets an intelligent coaching assistant, and every gym operator gets an operational copilot that helps run the business reliably.
Product objectives
1. Increase member engagement, workout consistency, and membership retention.
2. Reduce administrative work for gym staff and trainers.
3. Improve lead conversion, renewals, and revenue visibility for gym owners.
4. Provide adaptive, evidence-informed fitness guidance with human oversight.
5. Support single gyms and multi-location fitness networks through one extensible platform.
6. Build a consumer fitness identity that can eventually work across participating gyms and independent fitness providers.
7. Make AI behavior observable, measurable, permission-controlled, and safe.
Product boundaries
The first release is not intended to build proprietary fitness hardware, train a foundational AI model from scratch, deliver medical diagnosis, or operate physical gym equipment without validated integrations and safety controls.

The product should integrate with established providers wherever possible and invest its differentiation in its software, workflows, data models, and user experience.
2. Target customers and users









Segment A — Independent gyms

One to three locations, owner-managed operations, limited staff, recurring membership revenue.

Primary value: simpler administration, better lead follow-up, renewals and payment tracking.











Segment B — Gym chains and franchises

Multiple branches, regional management, shared memberships, centralized reporting and standardized operating procedures.

Primary value: consolidated intelligence, governance, automation and branch comparisons.











Segment C — Trainers and fitness professionals

Independent trainers, group instructors, nutrition professionals and remote coaches.

Primary value: client management, program delivery, progress reviews and paid services.











Segment D — Fitness consumers

Beginners, regular gym users, home exercisers and people seeking classes or personal training.

Primary value: one adaptive fitness experience across compatible services, devices and locations.

4. Functional requirements and module specifications
This section defines the required behavior of the platform. Each module should be implemented as a real, connected workflow, not merely as a set of screens.

Priority definitions:
- P0 — Launch critical: Required for a commercially usable first release.
- P1 — Differentiation: Required for the first major upgrade.
- P2 — Expansion: Requires additional integrations, data maturity or market validation.
4.1 Identity, organizations and tenant management
P0
ID	Requirement	Acceptance criteria
ID-01	Authentication	Users can register, sign in, recover accounts and terminate sessions securely.
ID-02	Organization onboarding	A business can create an organization, its first branch and an owner account.
ID-03	Multi-tenancy	All tenant-owned records are isolated between organizations.
ID-04	Staff invitations	Authorized managers can invite staff and assign predefined roles.
ID-05	Membership in organizations	A person can be a member at one gym and a trainer or employee at another, subject to separate permissions.
ID-06	Branch context	Staff see only authorized branches and can switch context where permitted.
ID-07	SaaS entitlements	Platform plans control access to features, usage quotas and integration limits.
ID-08	Auditability	Sensitive actions record actor, timestamp, target and outcome.

Additional behavior:
- Email and phone verification, with appropriate rate limits.
- Social sign-in and enterprise SSO as configurable options.
- Session revocation after account compromise or staff removal.
- Support-assisted recovery with identity verification.
- Invitation expiry and revocation.
- Organization suspension that does not silently delete its data.
- Subscription grace periods and read-only access rules.
- Data export and account deletion requests.
4.2 Membership and subscription engine
P0
Required entities include membership products, pricing versions, contracts, entitlements, member subscriptions, freezes, renewal schedules and payment records.

Core functionality:
- Configure membership duration, price, taxes, benefits, access hours and eligible branches.
- Sell memberships online and at reception.
- Record payment status separately from membership status.
- Support full and partial payments, where the chosen payment provider and business policy permit.
- Configure recurring billing and renewal consent.
- Calculate pro-rated changes using explicit, testable rules.
- Apply coupons, discounts and approval limits.
- Manage upgrades, downgrades, freezes, cancellations and reactivations.
- Generate receipts, invoices and credit notes.
- Configure trial memberships and guest passes.
- Support class credits and personal-training session packages.
- Track expiry, renewal eligibility and failed payments.
- Handle disputes and refunds without erasing the original financial record.
Critical business rule: A payment success event and a membership activation are separate operations. Activation must follow validated payment or an authorized complimentary/credit arrangement, and duplicate gateway notifications must not create duplicate memberships.
4.3 Booking and scheduling engine
P0
Support:
- Group classes, personal-training sessions, assessments, consultations and facility reservations.
- Recurring schedules and schedule exceptions.
- Instructor, room, equipment and branch availability.
- Capacity limits, eligibility rules, booking windows and cancellation policies.
- Waitlists and automatic promotion.
- Booking credits and package deductions.
- Time-zone-aware scheduling.
- Calendar synchronization.
- No-show and attendance records.
- Trainer substitution and customer notifications.
- Class utilization and booking demand reports.
Concurrency requirement: If one place remains in a class and two members attempt to book simultaneously, the system must allow no more than one successful reservation. Enforce this in the database and booking service, not merely in the interface.
4.4 Training and workout engine
P0 foundation; P1 adaptive AI
Build a structured exercise library with:
- Exercise identifiers, names, descriptions and localized labels.
- Target muscle groups, movement patterns and equipment requirements.
- Difficulty, coaching cues, contraindication notes and approved alternatives.
- Video and image assets with licensing metadata.
- Exercise variations and substitution relationships.
- Sets, repetitions, duration, distance, tempo, rest and intensity targets.
- Workout templates, training plans and scheduled sessions.
- Completed session records and individual set performance.
- Personal records and progress summaries.
- Trainer approvals and plan version history.
Workout lifecycle:

Draft → Review → Published → Scheduled → In progress → Completed

Allow cancellation or abandonment from applicable states. A plan change must not silently overwrite completed workout history.

The AI should create plans from approved templates and exercise constraints initially. Trainers must be able to review, edit and publish plans, especially when the member reports pain, a limitation, or a relevant medical restriction.
4.5 AI fitness coach and adaptive intelligence
P1
The AI fitness coach should be a persistent service with structured context, explicit capabilities, and an evaluation framework.

Inputs
- User goals and stated preferences.
- Training experience and available equipment.
- Completed and skipped workouts.
- Logged sets, repetitions, intensity and personal records.
- Self-reported fatigue, soreness and discomfort.
- Optional, authorized wearable data.
- Trainer-approved program constraints.
- Available workout time.
Outputs
- Recommended workout for the day.
- Exercise substitutions with explanations.
- Suggested progression or regression.
- Rest-day or lighter-session recommendations.
- Weekly progress summary.
- Goal adherence and consistency insights.
- Clarifying questions when data is insufficient.
- Escalation to a trainer or appropriate professional when needed.
Functional requirements
ID	Requirement	Acceptance criteria
AI-01	Context-aware recommendations	Uses relevant, authorized member data rather than generic prompts alone.
AI-02	Structured output	Workout recommendations conform to a validated schema.
AI-03	Constraint validation	Rejects plans that violate configured equipment, scheduling or safety constraints.
AI-04	Explainability	Shows the key factors behind a recommendation in understandable language.
AI-05	Human review	Trainer approval can be required by policy or risk category.
AI-06	Feedback loop	Records whether a recommendation was accepted, modified or rejected.
AI-07	Evaluation	Tracks quality, constraint violations, safety failures and user feedback.
AI-08	Model fallback	Handles model timeouts and unavailable providers gracefully.
AI-09	Privacy	Sends only the minimum necessary context to the selected AI provider.
AI-10	Versioning	Stores model, prompt/policy and recommendation versions for reproducibility.

Adaptive workout workflow
1. Collect permitted context

Goals, history, equipment, availability and optional recovery data

2. Generate candidate plan

Rules engine + AI recommendations

3. Validate constraints

Schema, progression limits, equipment and safety checks

4. Apply review policy

Automatic publication only for eligible low-risk recommendations; otherwise request approval

5. Observe outcomes and improve

Completion, exertion, feedback and future recommendation evaluation


Do not allow the language model to directly modify financial records, access credentials, or unrestricted production data. AI should request narrowly scoped application tools, and the backend should independently authorize and validate every action.

4.13 Business intelligence and AI operations copilot
P1
The business copilot should answer questions from authorized, current data and execute approved actions through controlled application tools.

Examples:
- “Which members have not visited for 14 days?”
- “Show renewals due in the next seven days.”
- “Which leads are overdue for follow-up?”
- “Which classes are underbooked compared with their history?”
- “Prepare the weekly branch performance report.”
- “Draft a renewal campaign for members who have consented to marketing.”
- “Create tasks for failed-payment follow-ups.”
- “Find equipment with repeated maintenance costs.”
Every answer involving business figures should identify its reporting period, definitions and data freshness.

Every action should have a preview, permission check, confirmation where necessary, execution record and outcome status. Financial transactions, mass communications, account suspension and access-control changes should require explicit authorization.

6. End-to-end business workflows
Workflow A — Lead to paying member
Lead captured
Deduplicate and assign owner
Consent-aware engagement and trial booking
Visit, sales follow-up and plan selection
Payment confirmation or authorized complimentary enrollment
Membership activated → onboarding → fitness plan

Business rules:
- A lead may exist without a member account.
- A member may exist without a paid subscription.
- A pending payment does not grant paid membership entitlements.
- A failed payment creates a follow-up or retry workflow, not a duplicate subscription.
- Each conversion retains attribution to its original lead and campaign.
Workflow B — Member books a class
1. Member selects a branch, class and session.
2. Backend checks eligibility, remaining credits, booking window and capacity.
3. Reservation is created atomically.
4. Payment or credit deduction is recorded when required.
5. Confirmation and reminder notifications are scheduled.
6. Member checks in using an authorized method.
7. Attendance and credit consumption are reconciled.
8. If a cancellation occurs, the applicable refund/credit policy runs.
9. A waitlisted member is promoted only if capacity and eligibility still permit it.
Workflow C — AI creates a daily workout
1. Load authorized member context and current training-plan version.
2. Determine equipment, schedule and applicable constraints.
3. Generate a candidate workout.
4. Validate the structured result against exercise and training rules.
5. Request trainer approval when required.
6. Publish the plan and explain relevant changes.
7. Record actual workout performance and feedback.
8. Evaluate the recommendation's quality and outcomes.
Workflow D — Renewal and retention
1. Identify memberships approaching expiry.
2. Evaluate renewal eligibility and available contact permissions.
3. Group members by actionable business signals.
4. Generate approved, personalized renewal recommendations.
5. Create sales tasks or send permitted messages.
6. Track replies, payments, renewals and opt-outs.
7. Measure renewal conversion and campaign performance.
8. Suppress further promotional messaging where required.
Workflow E — Autonomous operations
1. An application event or scheduled rule identifies an issue.
2. A workflow evaluates conditions and required permissions.
3. The system creates a proposed action.
4. Low-risk, reversible actions may run automatically under an approved policy.
5. High-impact actions require human confirmation.
6. The action runs through the authorized application service.
7. The result, retries and audit record are stored.
8. Failed actions are retried safely or sent to a review queue.
Examples of eligible low-risk automation include creating a task, preparing a report, or drafting a message. Refunds, bulk outreach, membership suspension and physical access changes require stricter controls.
7. Core data model and database design
Use PostgreSQL as the primary transactional database. The schema should be normalized around business entities, with JSONB reserved for genuinely variable configuration or provider-specific payloads—not as a substitute for relational design.

Data integrity requirements
- Use integer minor units or an appropriate fixed-precision decimal representation for monetary values; never binary floating-point for financial calculations.
- Store currency explicitly.
- Use UTC timestamps for events and preserve the relevant branch or user time zone for display.
- Enforce uniqueness on provider transaction identifiers and invoice numbers within the applicable scope.
- Use immutable financial event history and correction entries instead of silently overwriting historical transactions.
- Use idempotency keys for payment initiation and other non-repeatable business operations.

7.6 Database and storage conventions
- Use UUID identifiers where appropriate for distributed operations and external references.
- Include created_at and updated_at on mutable business entities.
- Add optimistic version fields to important editable records.
- Use foreign keys, unique constraints, checks and explicit state transitions.
- Partition large append-heavy event tables when measured growth justifies it.
- Archive or delete data according to documented retention policies.
- Keep large videos, images and exports in object storage, not PostgreSQL byte fields.
- Maintain separate analytical views or a warehouse when operational reporting starts affecting transactional performance.
- Never allow an AI-generated query to bypass application authorization and run unrestricted SQL against production.

This is the core technical differentiator and should receive its own design, testing and governance strategy.
9.1 AI service architecture

Fitness engine

Training rules, constraints, recommendations

Application services and audit

9.2 AI capability tiers
Tier	Capabilities	Execution policy
Tier 1 — Assist	Explain exercises, summarize records, draft messages	Respond using authorized context
Tier 2 — Recommend	Suggest workout changes, renewal follow-ups, class scheduling	Validate and present recommendations
Tier 3 — Prepare	Create draft plans, tasks, campaigns and reports	Save drafts, request approval where required
Tier 4 — Execute	Send approved messages, create permitted tasks, run scheduled reports	Enforce allowlists, permissions and audit logging
Tier 5 — Optimize	Adjust eligible workflows using measured outcomes	Controlled experiments, explicit policies and rollback

Tier 5 should not mean unrestricted self-modifying production behavior. Workflow definitions, permissions and safety policies should be versioned and governed independently of the AI model.
9.3 Fitness intelligence engine
Start with deterministic logic for safety-critical constraints and use AI for flexible reasoning and personalization.

The engine should combine:
1. Structured exercise and equipment catalog.
2. Trainer-defined templates and progression rules.
3. Member goals and approved constraints.
4. Workout history and feedback.
5. Optional wearable data.
6. A model gateway for recommendations.
7. A deterministic validation layer.
8. Outcome tracking and offline evaluations.
Do not rely on a language model to calculate all training loads, interpret every physiological signal, or guarantee a safe workout. Use explicit application logic for limits and constraints wherever possible.
9.4 AI evaluation framework
Maintain a test set that includes:
- Beginner and advanced training scenarios.
- Missing or contradictory member data.
- Equipment unavailable at the selected branch.
- Conflicting schedule and training-plan constraints.
- User-reported pain or other safety-sensitive input.
- Invalid model output.
- Prompt-injection attempts in member content.
- Cross-tenant data access attempts.
- Provider timeout and fallback scenarios.
- Unauthorized requests to perform business actions.
Measure:
- Schema validity.
- Constraint-violation rate.
- Safety-review outcomes.
- Recommendation acceptance and edit rates.
- Member satisfaction.
- Response latency and failure rate.
- Cost per completed AI task.
- Improvement against a baseline recommendation system.
Set release gates before enabling AI-generated plans for unsupervised use.
10. Security, privacy and safety requirements
Security must be built into the architecture and verified through tests—not added after the feature set is complete.
10.1 Identity and access security
- Enforce least privilege.
- Separate platform support roles from tenant business roles.
- Check object-level authorization for every sensitive record.
- Enforce tenant isolation in the application and database access layer.
- Use secure password hashing through a maintained authentication library.
- Protect session tokens and support revocation.
- Offer MFA for privileged accounts and enterprise customers.
- Rate-limit login, recovery and verification endpoints.
- Maintain audit trails for sensitive actions.
- Apply short-lived, narrowly scoped access for external integrations.
10.2 Data protection
- TLS for data in transit.
- Encryption at rest for databases, backups and object storage.
- Centralized secret management and rotation.
- Explicit data classifications for public, business, personal, health-related and highly restricted data.
- Data minimization and purpose limitation.
- Member-controlled connected-device permissions.
- Retention schedules and deletion workflows.
- Controlled exports and logged administrative access.
- Vendor due diligence for AI, messaging, analytics and payment providers.
- Documented breach response and recovery procedures.
10.3 Fitness and health safety
- Clearly distinguish wellness guidance from medical advice.
- Do not diagnose illness or claim guaranteed injury prevention.
- Provide a route to human review for relevant safety-sensitive situations.
- Do not encourage exercise through reported severe pain, faintness or other potentially urgent symptoms.
- Require qualified human approval for higher-risk programs according to the product's risk policy.
- Validate exercise instructions and media before publishing.
- Treat body measurements, health notes and progress photos as sensitive information.
- Provide clear controls over who can view or share this information.
10.4 Biometric and access-control safeguards
If face or fingerprint authentication is supported:
- Integrate with qualified access-control providers where practical.
- Document the purpose and legal basis for collection.
- Obtain applicable consent and provide appropriate alternatives.
- Minimize retention and avoid storing raw biometric images unless demonstrably necessary and lawful.
- Restrict access to biometric records and audit all sensitive operations.
- Define fallback procedures for false rejection, device failure and emergencies.
- Validate applicable Indian and other jurisdictional legal requirements before deployment.
10.5 Regulatory readiness
For an India-first product, the compliance workstream should cover:
- Applicable provisions and rules under India's DPDP framework.
- Consumer protection and membership cancellation policies.
- Applicable GST and invoicing requirements.
- Payment-provider obligations and PCI DSS scope.
- Applicable employment and payroll obligations for staff-related features.
- Electronic records, consent and communications requirements.
- Cross-border processing and retention obligations where relevant.
- Accessibility and platform-specific privacy requirements.
The applicable requirements should be verified against the law and rules in force at launch, with qualified legal and tax review. This document is not a legal compliance certification.

15. Testing and release acceptance criteria
Every module must have testable completion criteria.
Platform acceptance gates
- Tenant A cannot read or modify tenant B's records, even by changing identifiers in API requests.
- Staff permissions are enforced by the backend, not just hidden UI controls.
- Duplicate payment events cannot create duplicate charges or duplicate membership activations.
- Concurrent class bookings never exceed capacity.
- Failed notifications and integration events are retried or surfaced for resolution.
- Membership freezes, cancellations and refunds follow documented rules.
- Financial reports reconcile with the underlying transactions.
- AI-generated workouts conform to schemas and pass applicable constraint checks.
- AI business actions cannot bypass role permissions or approval requirements.
- Member consent withdrawal stops future processing for the relevant optional purpose as required by policy.
- Data export and deletion requests follow the documented retention and legal-hold rules.
- Backup restoration and disaster recovery are tested before launch.
Required automated test suites
1. Unit tests for domain logic and business rules.
2. Integration tests for database transactions and payment providers.
3. API contract tests.
4. Tenant-isolation and authorization tests.
5. End-to-end tests for member enrollment, payment, booking and attendance.
6. Concurrency tests for bookings and inventory.
7. AI evaluation and prompt-injection tests.
8. Mobile device and accessibility tests.
9. Performance and load tests.
10. Backup restoration and deployment rollback tests.

19. Final product strategy
The most defensible version of this platform is not the one with the most screens or the largest number of AI features.

It is the one that connects three feedback loops:
Fitness loop
Plan → Train → Record → Evaluate → Adapt

Improves the member's fitness experience using actual outcomes and appropriate oversight.


Business loop
Acquire → Onboard → Engage → Renew → Improve

Connects sales, memberships, attendance, retention and revenue into one operational workflow.


Intelligence loop
Observe → Recommend → Validate → Execute → Measure

Turns AI from a conversational interface into a measurable, controlled operational capability.
